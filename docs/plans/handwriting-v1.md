# Proposal: first Hebrew handwriting recognizer

Status: research plan, 20 November 2026; no training or evaluation authorized by this document. Run in parallel with the niqqud track, with separate people, data and budget reservations; niqqud retains priority. The target is modern cursive line recognition. The existing held-out ivrit.ai benchmark has 225 lines from 10 writers; the owner reports roughly 75–83% character error for the readers tried. This is the baseline to beat, not a measurement made for this proposal. A handwriting v1 has not been measured on a held-out set yet.

Other leads need restrictions, not silent inclusion. [ivrit.ai's public benchmark card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ivrit-ai/hebrew-handwriting-ocr-benchmark) describes two configurations, lines and pages, **test only**, with 225 verified transcriptions, 10 pages and 10 writer fingerprints; there is no train split. Its `submitter_fingerprint` is a proxy, not a proven universal writer ID. It is gated; the card calls its licence the ivrit.ai licence. The published [licence](https://www.ivrit.ai/en/the-license/) has v1 and v2, both CC BY 4.0 with overriding purpose restrictions: v1 only AI training; v2 AI training or academic research; both restrict deepfakes and v2 adds export/sanctions restrictions. The card does not establish which version applies to each upstream batch. These are not unrestricted CC BY terms; no ivrit.ai material is admitted as training data under this plan. The collection [application](https://github.com/ivrit-ai/ivrit-handwriting-collection) is not a public training dataset. Do not retrieve benchmark images, labels or rows.

[HHD v0](https://tc11.cvc.uab.es/datasets/HHD_v0_1) is CC BY-ND 3.0, so exclude it and derivatives unless a separate open grant is established. [DiffusionPen Hebrew](https://huggingface.co/datasets/cyttic/diffusionpen-hebrew-handwriting/blob/main/README.md) advertises 149,952 synthetic labelled lines, 491 styles and writer-independent splits (119,986/14,855/15,111), but its metadata says MIT while its card says CC BY 4.0 and disclaims rights over the literary text; style provenance is not established. Exclude pending resolution of both rights and writer overlap. [IAM](https://fki.tic.heia-fr.ch/databases/iam-handwriting-database) is English, not a Hebrew collection; do not rely on permissive mirror labels when original terms are restricted. [BiblIA](https://github.com/itayinbarr/heb-ocr#data-and-licensing) is described as CC BY-NC-SA, so exclude it. These leads do not count toward the open training inventory.

## Leakage and data acquisition gates

The lead supplies the protected writer list at training time: **`<HELD_OUT_WRITER_LIST_SUPPLIED_BY_LEAD>`**. Keep it private and out of public artifacts. Exclude **every line, page, glyph and synthetic style derived from those 10 writers**, including other submissions and alternate identifiers, from training, validation, pretraining and model initialization. Obtain a trusted mapping from source writer identifiers and submitter fingerprints to that list; fingerprint is not necessarily writer identity. Reject an entire source or item when identity, mapping, provenance or overlap cannot be established. Include hashes and near-duplicate checks across admissible data, source versions and any independently registered evaluation exclusions without opening test material. Freeze and audit a writer-disjoint train/dev split, with separate text and document groups, before any runs. Do not inspect or use test data for data selection, debugging, early stopping or retuning. Check pretrained checkpoints' full data provenance under the same rules; otherwise do not initialize from them.

Modern line-labelled data is the blocking gap: commission openly licensed, consented new contributions from writers proven disjoint from the protected list. Aim for 2,000–5,000 verified real modern lines from at least 50 diverse writers, with 10–20% of writers held for development; double-key transcription and adjudication, segmentation review, privacy screening and per-item rights records. These are planning targets, not a claim of sufficiency. Start a feasibility pilot with 500–1,000 real lines and 20–30 writers; if no eligible writers or labels exist, stop at data collection rather than implying synthetic-only support.

## Model and integration

| Option | Tradeoffs |
| --- | --- |
| Tesseract LSTM fine-tune | Familiar existing runtime and CPU inference; [Tesseract](https://github.com/tesseract-ocr/tesseract) is Apache-2.0. A retrainable float `tessdata_best` `heb` is needed; [fast/integer models are not fine-tunable](https://tesseract-ocr.github.io/tessdoc/Data-Files). Verify the exact `heocr_modern_v1` checkpoint is retrainable and its lineage/rights, otherwise use a verified eligible `heb` or do not fine-tune. CPU `lstmtraining` can take many hours; no GPU assumption. A print prior may help with little data but may bias cursive. |
| Kraken/ketos HTR | [Apache-2.0](https://github.com/mittagessen/kraken/blob/main/LICENSE); GPU training, CPU inference possible. Explicit line recognition and [ketos training](https://kraken.re/main/user_guide/training_recognition.html) fit variable handwriting. HeOCR already has a [Kraken baseline and training interface](../../README.md); the existing training contract's initialization restrictions must be respected or changed by a separate decision. From-scratch training needs more labelled data; any initialization needs provenance and licence audit. |
| TrOCR-style transformer | Flexible pretraining but larger GPU/CPU and memory costs, decoding and dependencies; checkpoint, tokenizer, source data and model licence require independent clearance. An adapter must take line crops and emit raw logical text via the same contract. No eligible checkpoint is established here. |

Recommend **Kraken/ketos**, initially from scratch with eligible synthetic pretraining then real-line fine-tuning only if the training contract and maintainer decision permit it. It matches the existing segmented-line workflow and avoids assuming a third-party checkpoint is clean. Keep Tesseract as a low-integration-cost comparator on the same development split, contingent on an eligible float checkpoint. Freeze the chosen model before tests. HeOCR's local pipeline supplies segmented line images/baselines to a Kraken recognition stage; preserve reading order and raw engine output, provide pinned local model hashes, and leave normalization to the evaluator. Validate Hebrew RTL, punctuation, blank outputs, line boundaries, CPU latency and memory with non-test fixtures. Use optional dependency packaging; no downloads or network inference at runtime. See [stage contracts](../stages.md) and [normalization boundary](../../planning/decisions/0002-normalization-boundary.md).

## Synthetic mix

Render 50,000–150,000 distinct CC0/public-domain text lines with Gveret Levin and any additional verified OFL handwriting fonts, through [hocrsyngen](https://github.com/HeOCR/hocrsyngen) or an equivalent reproducible RTL renderer. Verify glyph shaping, logical-order labels, non-clipping, punctuation and missing glyphs. Pin font, licence, text source, seed and renderer hash. Add controlled slant, baseline drift, spacing/scale variation, ink thickness, blur, compression, lighting, paper and mild rotation; keep a clean fraction and reject illegible crops. Avoid simulated new writer identities and leakage from protected handwriting. Font images are weak proxies for real writing. Pretrain on synthetic, fine-tune with real and a small synthetic replay mix; tune proportions on writer-disjoint development writers only. The proposed 2–5k real versus 50–150k synthetic lines is an initial budget, not a proven optimal ratio.

## Compute and cost

No training in this task. Lead queues all heavy jobs via [heocr-compute](https://github.com/HeOCR/heocr-compute): remote preparation/rendering and training, checkpoints, logs and allowed aggregate outputs only. The runner allows only configured open roots and a mandatory denylist; confirm that eligible licensed inputs fit its policy, update policy through a separate reviewed change if needed, and never upload held-out material. Its manifest hashes inputs, reserves worst-case spend under the $150/month runner cap, enforces wall-time, terminates pods and verifies downloads. Account-wide spend outside the runner is not counted. The Mac handles short reviews, manifests, exclusion-list matching, orchestration and small offline contract checks; the lead retains test custody and runs the final evaluation in its approved environment.

Budget estimate, not a benchmark: one GPU pilot 4–8 hours, synthetic pretraining 10–20 hours, real fine-tuning and up to two development reruns 10–20 hours, total **24–48 GPU-hours**. [RunPod's RTX 4090 page](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-models/rtx-4090) lists Community from $0.34/h and Secure $0.74/h; at $0.74/h this is $17.76–$35.52 compute, plus storage, transfer, setup and retries. Reserve $50–$70 after live quote and account-wide budget check, leaving at least $80 for niqqud/other work; stop if prices or measured time exceed the reservation. Tesseract comparator is remote CPU and separately quoted; allow 10–30 CPU-hours with a $10 provisional cap, subject to actual instance rate. No performance or cost is guaranteed.

## Evaluation and decision

Register both sets and the normalization/scoring protocol before any model is selected. Use development signals for selection only; never report development metrics as performance, even with caveats. Freeze checkpoint, dependencies, segmentation, decoding and comparator before the evaluator scores **once** on existing ivrit.ai lines. Use exactly the recorded baseline's line input, character normalization and aggregation; obtain its exact configuration and per-reader numbers from the lead before scoring. Report paired micro CER (total edits / reference characters), baseline delta, per-writer results and writer-cluster bootstrap interval; also median line CER and blank rate with explicit handling. Keep blank predictions in primary CER. Report small-sample uncertainty, and any mismatch with the baseline protocol rather than claiming a direct comparison. No test-driven iteration.

For an independently registered second set, reserve **all Pinkas**, including its nominal training split and all derivatives, from the outset. Its existing PAGE line gold makes a second historical handwriting test possible without new transcription. The evaluator must check rights, line geometry, normalization and existing gold quality before registration; do not inspect it during training. It is historical and cannot establish modern handwriting performance; writers are not identified, so avoid claims of writer independence. If the owner instead requires a second modern set, collect new disjoint consented writers and human line transcription plus independent adjudication; no eligible ready-labelled modern set has been established.
