# P-hist: candidate held-out evaluation without full transcription

Status: proposal, 2026-11-20. The candidate editions await eval's reservation check. This plan records source metadata and proposed methods only.

## What can be measured

HeOCR reports evaluation results only on registered, held-out real-world examples drawn in roughly the proportions in which users encounter them. Part A is the evaluation track. Part B uses generated pages for development and model selection.

Existing digital editions can locate passages on scans. Edition changes, skipped commentaries, spelling, marks and transcription errors can all be mistaken for OCR errors. Use the digital text to propose references, then have humans adjudicate a small, prespecified sample against the page images. Keep protected scans and their references out of development. The official 22-book test still requires its own references. [Real-page reference requirements](../../planning/decisions/0012-real-page-scored-sets.md)

## Part A. Real old-print evaluation

### Target population and proportions

Target the Hebrew body pages a user encounters when digitizing open historical printed books from roughly 1500–1900. Include rabbinic layouts with parallel commentaries, ordinary book prose, and the natural variation in print and scan condition. The unit is a physical page with Hebrew text. Include unaligned, faint and difficult pages in the sampling frame. Covers without body text, blank pages and manuscripts fall outside this population.

Use these provisional page proportions to budget acquisition and human review. They are deliberately rounded planning assumptions about digitization use. The 19th century gets most pages because the working assumption is that later editions account for more of the available scans users encounter. The genre mix emphasizes rabbinic books while giving liturgy and secular prose meaningful shares. Square and unpointed pages are expected to be most common across those genres. Mixed layouts and pointed Bible and liturgy need enough pages to measure their distinct OCR challenges.

| Sampling axis | Initial share of pages |
|---|---|
| Period | 15% 1500–1699, 20% 1700–1799, 65% 1800–1900 |
| Typeface | 55% predominantly square, 20% predominantly Rashi, 25% mixed square and Rashi |
| Genre | 15% Bible, 20% Talmud, 15% standalone commentary, midrash or rabbinic philosophy, 25% halakha and responsa, 10% liturgy, 15% secular and Haskalah prose |
| Pointing | 65% predominantly unpointed, 20% predominantly pointed, 15% with substantial pointed and unpointed text |

Treat these as marginal targets for the same pages. Keep plausible combinations, such as pointed square Bible text beside unpointed Rashi commentary. Assign genre by the edition's primary work, so a Talmud page with Rashi stays Talmud. For script and pointing, classify a page as predominant when roughly four fifths or more of its Hebrew body text uses that script or marking practice. Record script and pointing by region on mixed pages and record occasional niqqud and cantillation separately.

Before fixing the weights, eval should draw a small catalog sample across Commons, Internet Archive and HathiTrust full-view listings, including books that lack an available transcription. Deduplicate editions and digitizations, estimate eligible body-page counts, and classify script and pointing on a few randomly drawn body pages per sampled edition. Use the counts to estimate the joint page mix in the accessible catalog frame. Document archive and catalog coverage, the sample size and uncertainty, and where the clean benchmark pool falls short. Replace the planning shares with catalog-informed targets where the evidence warrants it and freeze the targets before scoring. Keep the catalog sample independent of model output and alignment feasibility.

Draw pages from multiple cleared editions per major stratum. Cap individual editions and presses and track shared works across reprints. Compare the final distribution with the frozen targets. If a major category lacks enough references, expand the pool, adjudicate harder pages, or narrow the stated population before reporting a result. A score on easier matches alone would describe a limited subset. Publish the population, target and achieved shares, coverage, and any exclusions with the registered evaluation. [Domain and sampling rules](../../planning/decisions/0017-domain-separated-evaluation.md)

## Registration and alignment protocol

1. Lead checks every full work, edition, scan and text against the private reservation list and all existing training, validation, pretraining, glyph banks and model initialization, including alternate editions and near duplicates. A previously contaminated work is ineligible; do not try to repair it by holding out different pages. Reserve accepted whole works and their derivatives against **all future training** before any construction. Record source IDs, exact revisions, licences and hashes in a private manifest; do not expose protected pages to workers. If origin or rights cannot be established, reject the item.
2. Freeze eligibility, sampling, region scope, matching parameters and failure handling before test inspection. Select pages independently of alignment confidence. Develop alignment on separate works. The evaluation custodian can use existing scan text or a frozen independent recognizer to locate verse or section anchors, then fuzzy-match local page windows in sequence. Use confidence and the margin over alternative matches to detect repeats, omissions and reordered pages. Preserve raw text, original Unicode, page IDs and coordinates. Record all sampled pages, failed matches and dispositions by stratum. Send failed matches to human review so that the reference method does not quietly remove difficult pages.
3. Use a blind random audit across books, script, quality and match-confidence bands, including failures; independent human comparison to the scan checks complete selected regions, page boundaries, order, omissions and punctuation, with adjudication. Use the image as the source of truth. To estimate review effort, start with three random pages per cleared edition and include all low-confidence pages in the proposed sample. Set the final audit size and acceptable residual reference error before scoring. Keep the random audit and low-confidence review counts distinct and stop if the reference check fails. For partial regions report coverage and exclusions explicitly; full-page metrics require complete page references and reading order. A small adjudicated subset can become gold after registration, while the remainder is unscored silver.
4. Freeze model, scorer, segmentation, normalization and all thresholds before the custodian scores once. Follow [decision 0017](../../planning/decisions/0017-domain-separated-evaluation.md): CER, WER, line exact rate and CER, page CER median/p90 and fixed-threshold shares, plus reading-order/layout cost where references support them; stratify square/Rashi/mixed and pointed/unpointed. Cluster intervals by source or edition and account for shared works and type settings across editions. Report sparse strata descriptively. Report denominators and coverage. No reported result is promised until a registered set and suitable references exist.

## Real evaluation track for old printed pointed Hebrew

### Population and provisional page mix

Target historical printed pages from roughly 1500–1940 with a readable body-text region in pointed Hebrew. Include facing translations, liturgical directions, marginalia and commentary as layout conditions. Exclude manuscript pages and pages whose only Hebrew pointing is on a title, running head or isolated word. Record region coverage so a page with pointed square text beside unpointed Rashi commentary can contribute to the mixed-layout stratum. Whole-page scores require references for all regions in the scoring profile.

| Axis | Initial target share of pages |
|---|---|
| Period | 10% 1500–1699, 15% 1700–1799, 45% 1800–1899, 30% 1900–1940 |
| Typeface | 80% predominantly square, 18% mixed square and Rashi, 2% predominantly Rashi |
| Genre | 30% daily and Sabbath prayer books, 25% machzorim and Seliḥot, 25% Bibles and chumashim, 10% Passover Haggadot, 10% children's and educational books |
| Niqqud density | 70% dense, 25% mixed, 5% sparse with a coherent pointed Hebrew region |

These rounded shares are planning assumptions. Bible and liturgy supply most of the expected pointed pages and mostly use square type. Rashi is more likely in commentary on mixed pages than in the main pointed stream. Later editions receive greater weight because more widely circulated 19th- and early 20th-century editions are expected in the accessible scan supply. Keep the early and mixed strata large enough to check their distinct layouts and type. Do not fill a liturgy or children's quota with dictionary entries or isolated passages.

As in Part A, estimate the actual joint mix from a separate sample of eligible open catalog holdings, including editions that lack a transcription. Deduplicate copies and facsimiles, sample body pages from different presses and rites, and weight by estimated eligible page counts. Measure how common bilingual facing pages, parallel commentary, damaged scans, and each density band are. Freeze catalog-informed shares and edition caps before selecting evaluation pages. Preserve common joint combinations and report gaps. If the cleared pool cannot support a material stratum, expand it or scope the reported population to supported strata.

### Edition inventory and reference construction

The [pointed-print inventory](phist-old-pointed-candidates.csv) records the scan route, scan and text rights, edition match, scoped coverage, and a 1–3 feasibility judgment using the Part A scale. Wikisource references tied to the scan are preferable because the page boundaries already exist. They can still omit lines, regularize marks, or contain unchecked text. Of 23 distinct edition records, five have scan-linked pointed prayer or Bible pages and eight have scoped or conditional evidence. The other ten are leads with unresolved scan, reference, or pointing coverage. Start the reservation check with the five `pointed-pages` rows and review the `pointed-region-conditional` rows for the specific scope in the CSV. The inventory still falls short of 15 confirmed in-scope, same-edition pairs. Early print, Passover Haggadot and machzorim are the largest gaps. Hard-to-match liturgy can add breadth once an allowed scan of the exact edition is found, for example the separately transcribed Venice 1560 [Maḥzor Aram Ṣoba](https://opensiddur.org/compilations/liturgical/mahzorim/mahzor-aram-tsoba/). A modern re-vocalization, a rite match, and a modern text of the same biblical book can help locate a passage. Each needs image-matched adjudication before it can serve as a scored reference.

Counts are digitized file pages, including covers and untranscribed leaves. For Internet Archive records use the published image count or the scan metadata leaf count. Keep the original and later added material distinct when documenting rights, and pin the exact text revision. Preserve the attribution terms on Wikisource and Open Siddur texts.

Niqqud density is the percentage of Hebrew lexical tokens bearing at least one vowel point in the stated region. Dense is at least 80%, mixed is 20–79%, and sparse is below 20%. The inventory records small digital-text spot samples or qualitative expectations where marked. A region estimate does not establish the density of the page or print run. The numerical spot checks count Unicode vowel points in the rendered Wikisource text and may include headings or annotations. The custodian records image-checked density over all Hebrew body tokens on each sampled page and density by region. Count a dot in vav as a vowel when it is shuruq, and track consonantal dagesh, shin and sin dots, and cantillation separately. Exclude Aramaic, Yiddish, Judeo-Arabic, and other facing-language content from the Hebrew density denominator. Keep these regions in the layout inventory.

No Project Ben-Yehuda source is proposed for this track. Exclude all 22 NQ1 Ben-Yehuda works across editions and digital sources, and exclude Ezekiel, Ezra, and Nehemiah, including their extracts in lectionaries, prayer books and lesson material. Use allowlisted passages in Bible rows. Eval must check the remaining work identities, alternate titles, parallel texts and scan files against NQ1 and the other private reservations before any acquisition or reference construction. Reserve accepted sources from future training according to the registration protocol above.

Align through scan-leaf IDs where available. Otherwise anchor by verse, prayer sequence, folio or lesson, then fuzzy-match within the corresponding page windows. Match a marks-stripped copy for locating text and carry the original marked reference through to adjudication. Repeated prayers and quotations need multiple neighboring anchors. In the audit, check marks and consonants against the image, including qamats variants, missing or unclear vowels, and qere/ketiv. Keep a record of transcriber-supplied points, damaged marks, unreadable spans and page boundaries. Let the image determine the reference. Resolve failures on the prespecified sample instead of silently dropping harder pages.

Freeze Unicode canonical normalization and how to treat damaged marks before scoring. Report consonant CER, marked Hebrew grapheme error, and vowel insertion, deletion and substitution rates on aligned base letters. State the denominator and track base-letter errors separately. Report cantillation separately where supported. Follow the main registration protocol for adjudication, layout metrics and uncertainty clustered by edition or shared setting. This track reports results only on registered real pages with checked references. No OCR, reference construction, or evaluation was performed for this inventory.

## Part B. Does a direction generalise to unseen fonts?

Use these renders only for development and model selection. Compare candidate training directions on the same validation pages to see whether a change generalises beyond the font families used in training. A gain here with no gain on representative real old print counts for nothing. HeOCR reports performance only from Part A or another registered, representative real set. Freeze model selection before the custodian opens the real evaluation results.

Use verified public-domain Hebrew prose and rabbinic passages from separate pinned transcriptions. Record the underlying work, transcription edition and licence. For Project Ben-Yehuda, require the item-level public-domain mark and historic edition provenance. Keep passages and near duplicates disjoint from training and real evaluation works. Reserve text, layout variants, seeds and entire font families for validation before rendering. Cross square, Rashi and mixed commentary layouts with clean, mild and strong degradation. Within each populated cell, draw distinct source passages across sizes, column counts and abbreviation density. Keep logical RTL labels, line and region boxes, and reading order. Verify shaping, marks, fallback and glyph coverage on separate calibration inputs. Derive validation labels from the renderer input. [Ben-Yehuda reuse terms](https://benyehuda.org/page/about)

## Eval handoff

The lead passes the CSV to eval for the private reservation sweep. After clearance, confirm scan rights and page coverage, check the proposed real page mix against the catalog sample, and register the adjudicated real page set when it meets the reference and representation requirements. Keep the font validation workflow with development. Assign construction and any heavy computation through the lead.
